Where High-Power Passives Fail

A 200 W attenuator is a thermal component that happens to have an RF function. Heat has to leave the resistive element and reach the housing before it reaches the connector.

Block 01

Resistive element

Thin-film or thick-film on a thermally conductive substrate. The element geometry sets both the attenuation value and the heat density it must survive.

Block 02

Die attach and substrate

The die attachment and substrate carry heat into the metal housing. A void here shows up later as drift or an open circuit under load.

Block 03

Housing and fins

Machined housing with fins or a mounting pad. The interface to the bench or cold plate is part of the rating and is stated on the datasheet.

Linear performance matters as much as thermal performance. Intermodulation values down to −160 dBc and lower are quoted for defined two-tone conditions, and the test condition is part of the number. Divider isolation follows the network topology as much as the split ratio; the Wilkinson power divider guide explains how isolation and split loss are read separately.

A Calibration Network Is a Tracking Problem

High-Power Passive Questions

Five questions asked before a power component is ordered.

Is the power rating continuous or peak?

The rating is stated for a defined mounting and ambient condition, and that condition is on the datasheet. A part used without the intended heat sink or airflow will not carry the published figure. Where the application is pulsed, the duty cycle and pulse width are supplied so the thermal case can be checked.

Why does insertion loss exclude the split?

A two-way divider inherently halves the power, which is 3 dB. Quoting insertion loss excluding the split separates the design's own loss from that unavoidable division, so an eight-way divider can be compared with another eight-way divider on equal terms.

Can a calibration network be built for another band?

Yes, when the band and the feed interface are defined. The published build covers 1.7–6 GHz with N female connectors and IP55. A different band or connector changes the internal coupler and the housing, so those are confirmed before quotation.

Do you supply waveguide components on this page?

Only the V-band 39–50 GHz parts are listed here. Straight, curved and twisted sections, couplers and transitions are catalogued on the waveguide components page so the flange and section tables stay in one place.

How is linearity specified?

As intermodulation for a defined two-tone test, with values to −160 dBc and lower depending on the part. The test condition — tone spacing and power per tone — is part of the number; without it, an IM figure cannot be compared with another supplier's.
