06 / Review notes5 questions

Engineering and Quality Review FAQs

Direct answers about audits, test capabilities, review inputs, expected outputs and the difference between capacity and timing.

Can buyers audit SONNE's factory and quality processes?

Factory audit and visit requests can be reviewed around a specific project. The agenda may cover relevant machining, assembly, testing, traceability and change-control records. Timing, accessible areas, confidentiality and required documentation should be agreed in advance rather than assumed. Contact SONNE with your audit scope so the appropriate review path can be confirmed.

What RF testing capabilities are available?

SONNE's stated capabilities include VNA, PIM, reliability and multi-port matrix automated testing. The applicable measurements, conditions, sample coverage and report content depend on the product and agreed specification. Buyers should identify critical RF parameters and acceptance criteria during engineering review so the verification plan and evidence requirements can be defined.

What inputs are needed to start an engineering review?

Provide the application, project stage and the information already available: frequency or band plan, RF targets, power or PIM needs, mechanical envelope, connectors, drawings, environment and quantity range. Unknown items can be marked as open. SONNE can then identify missing inputs, review feasibility questions and recommend the next design, prototype or testing step.

What should an engineering review deliver before prototyping?

A useful review should leave a controlled record of known requirements, open questions, assumptions, major mechanical or RF risks and the proposed verification scope. Depending on the project, it may also include drawing or DFM comments and a prototype input baseline. It should not be presented as a performance guarantee before hardware has been tested.

Does annual capacity guarantee a production lead time?

No. SONNE states annual capacity above 500,000 units and more than 50 high-precision CNC machines, but those figures do not determine a specific delivery date. Production timing must be evaluated from the drawing, quantity, process route, verification requirements and current capacity. Prototype and mass-production schedules should therefore be confirmed separately for each project.
