Prototype, Quote and Lead-Time Boundaries

Response time, quotation, prototype and production timing are separate decisions. Treating them as one promise is the most common source of misunderstanding.

  1. 01

    Complete RFQ

    Product line, band plan or drawing, targets, quantity and verification needs are submitted.

  2. 02

    Initial feedback

    About 24 hours after a complete RFQ; feasibility questions and missing inputs are identified.

  3. 03

    Clarification

    Open parameters, revision status, test scope and commercial inputs are resolved with engineering.

  4. 04

    Quotation

    Price and commercial terms are reviewed only after the technical and quantity baseline is clear.

  5. 05

    Prototype and production

    Prototype feasibility and production timing are assessed per project from the confirmed inputs.

Prototype reference

Approximately two weeks is a reference for suitable prototype projects only. It is not a general prototype or production commitment, and suitability is confirmed during engineering review.

Production timing

Production lead time is assessed from drawings, quantities, process route, verification requirements and available capacity. Annual capacity is not a delivery date.

Quotation input

Submit the known technical baseline and quantity range. A quotation follows clarification rather than the initial engineering response.

Plan the review, not a date: the 24-hour and two-week statements describe different review stages. Neither one books production capacity or confirms a shipment date.

Quality and Verification Evidence

Test capability becomes useful evidence only when the method, conditions, sample or batch and acceptance decision are recorded together.

What the evidence should connect

A report is reviewable when it links the product or part number, revision, test conditions, measured values and the acceptance decision. A curve without that context is difficult to use in a design review.

Where 100% verification applies

A 100% verification statement is meaningful only for the specific product and critical parameter agreed in the project. It does not extend to every product or every test in the portfolio.

How to request evidence

Name the parameters that matter for your design, the sample or batch scope, and the report fields your quality team needs. Engineering can then confirm the verification route and the documents available for the project.

Confidentiality and Custom RF Manufacturing FAQs

NDA scope, drawing handling, compliance documents and after-sales routes depend on the specific project. The five answers below state the condition and the next confirmation step.

Before sensitive drawings are exchanged

Start with a non-confidential overview where practical. Then agree the disclosure route, the recipients, the permitted use and the handling requirements for the files in writing.

  • Name the document types and revisions to be shared.
  • Identify who may receive and review each file.
  • Confirm whether an NDA is required for this project.
  • Agree how controlled files are transmitted and retained.

Quality issues and after-sales

Report the order, lot or serial identification, measured evidence and operating conditions. That lets engineering review the issue and agree containment or corrective action. Warranty and RMA scope, timing and remedies are defined by the project agreement rather than assumed from this page.

Documents that are not asserted here

RoHS, REACH, salt-spray and EMC documentation is confirmed per product and project where required. This page does not claim that every product carries every document.

What frequency range can SONNE support?

Core production covers DC to 40 GHz. Selected custom microwave and waveguide projects can reach 110 GHz, depending on topology and verification. This is not a universal range for every product. Submit frequency bands, interfaces, mechanical constraints and required test evidence for project-specific engineering review.

What is the MOQ for custom RF components?

There is no universal MOQ across product lines. MOQ depends on the product family, customization, process route and project quantity. The public MOQ 50+ statement applies only where specified for the IBS/DAS line, not all SONNE products. Submit drawings or specifications and quantities for project confirmation.

How quickly can I receive feedback, a quote and delivery?

A complete RFQ can usually receive initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. This is not a quotation or delivery guarantee. Prototypes in approximately two weeks are only a reference where applicable. Production timing is assessed from drawings, quantities, processes, verification requirements and available capacity.

Can custom drawings be protected by an NDA?

Confidentiality scope and NDA terms must be agreed for the specific project. Begin with a non-confidential overview where practical, then share controlled drawings through the agreed route. Do not assume unrestricted disclosure or a blanket NDA. Define recipients, permitted use and handling requirements before exchanging sensitive design files.

How are quality problems and after-sales issues handled?

Report the order, lot or serial identification, measured evidence and operating conditions so engineering can review the issue and agree containment or corrective action. Applicable VNA, PIM or reliability evidence should support the analysis. Warranty and RMA scope, timing and remedies must be defined per project agreement, not assumed as a blanket policy.
