07 / Device notes5 questions

RF Combiner, Duplexer and Diplexer FAQs

Clarify device functions, multi-band trade-offs, band-plan readiness and low-PIM verification. For further terminology reading, use the planned Insights resource route.

What is the difference between a duplexer and a diplexer?

A duplexer commonly enables transmit and receive paths to share a common antenna or port while providing the required isolation. A diplexer separates or combines different frequency paths. Because usage can vary by system, define every port, frequency band and signal direction in the RFQ rather than relying on the device name alone.

How do I balance insertion loss and isolation in a multi-band combiner?

Evaluate both against the complete band plan, including port-to-port isolation targets, guard bands, power and mechanical constraints. Lower insertion loss and higher isolation can compete with bandwidth, size and topology choices. Specify which ports and frequency directions each isolation value applies to, then let engineering assess the trade-offs through design and verification.

Do I need a complete band plan to request a combiner quote?

A complete band plan is strongly recommended because frequency ranges, bandwidth, signal direction, power and isolation determine the topology. You can start with incomplete information if you identify unknown fields and describe the system connections. A complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours; quotation and delivery timing require further project review.

How is an RF power divider different from a combiner?

An RF power divider splits a signal into multiple paths, with amplitude, phase and port requirements defined for the application. A frequency-selective combiner brings different frequency bands into a common path. Some functions can be bidirectional depending on the network, so provide the actual signal directions, frequencies and port relationships instead of choosing by name alone.

How is low PIM verified for duplexers and combiners?

Low-PIM verification should define the product, ports, frequency bands, test setup, power conditions, PIM limit and acceptance criteria. SONNE lists low-PIM batch verification and matrix-switch automated testing capabilities. The exact coverage and reporting are agreed by product and project, so request the relevant test evidence rather than assuming one condition applies to every unit or model.
