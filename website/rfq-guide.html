How to Prepare an RFQ Engineers Can Quote Directly

An actionable quotation starts with a specification an engineer can review. This checklist organizes the frequency, performance, interface, mechanical, environmental, quantity and verification fields that belong in a custom RF, waveguide or enclosure RFQ.

  • Frequency and bandwidth
  • Insertion loss
  • Rejection and isolation
  • Power
  • PIM
  • Connectors
  • Mechanical envelope
  • Environment
  • Quantity
  • Verification

A complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. That feedback is not a formal quotation, a prototype commitment or a production lead-time promise.

RF Specification Checklist

Every value should carry a unit, a test condition and a note on whether it is mandatory or a target. A model number or a single frequency rarely defines a custom part.

Units, conditions and priority

Insertion loss without band edges or a return-loss reference cannot be compared between suppliers. State whether each limit applies across the full band, at a single frequency or at a temperature extreme, and group the fields by priority.

  • Required: values the design cannot be reviewed without.
  • Target: desirable performance used to compare options.
  • TBD: not yet known, so engineering can flag the missing input instead of guessing.

PIM and rejection need their own context

For PIM, give the order, tone spacing, power per carrier and port definition. For out-of-band rejection, give the frequency range and attenuation target for each band that must be blocked. Low-PIM and rejection fields are only meaningful with those conditions attached.

Quantity, Verification and Change-Control Inputs

Test scope and quantity belong in the first RFQ. Adding them after a quotation usually reopens the technical and commercial review.

Quantity and project stage

State whether the request is for a prototype, a pilot run or mass production, and give the target quantity and expected annual demand. That lets engineering separate the sample build from the production assessment.

Tests, limits and reporting

Name the critical parameters, the acceptance limits, the sample coverage and the required report format. VNA, PIM, reliability and matrix testing are defined against the product and its parameters; a 100% verification requirement should name the product and measurement it applies to.

Change control

Record the drawing or specification revision with the RFQ. If a requirement changes during review, note what changed, why and which test evidence must be repeated so the production baseline stays traceable.

Mechanical addendum

For enclosure and waveguide work, add the drawing revision, critical dimensions, GD&T callouts, material, surface finish, sealing or shielding requirement and the interfaces that must align with mating parts. Mark every value that is not yet fixed as TBD.

RFQ Submission and Preparation FAQs

How completeness, response time, lead time and confidentiality are handled before a quotation is issued.

What information is required for a custom RF filter RFQ?

Provide frequency range and bandwidth, insertion loss, return loss, rejection or isolation, power, PIM where applicable, impedance, connectors, mechanical envelope, environment, quantity and verification needs. Mark unknown items as TBD so engineering can identify gaps instead of treating a model number as a complete specification.

Can SONNE evaluate a project before every parameter is final?

Yes. Submit the known frequency range, target performance, mechanical constraints and open questions. Engineering can identify missing inputs and discuss feasibility before the specification is final. Initial engineering feedback is distinct from a formal quote, and prototype or production timing still depends on the agreed scope and requirements.

Does a 24-hour response include a quotation and lead time?

No. Approximately 24 hours refers to initial engineering feedback after a complete RFQ. It may clarify feasibility, missing inputs or next steps. Quotation and delivery timing require further review of drawings, quantities, processes, verification and capacity, so they are not guaranteed by the initial-response statement.

How are prototype and production lead times estimated?

The approximately two-week figure is only a reference for applicable prototype projects, not a general promise. Production lead time depends on drawings, quantities, process route, verification requirements and available capacity. Confirm timing after engineering and commercial review rather than deriving it from annual capacity or a prototype example.

Can drawings be reviewed under an NDA?

Confidentiality scope and NDA terms must be agreed for the specific project. Start with a non-confidential overview where practical, then exchange controlled drawings through the agreed route. Do not assume a blanket NDA, disclosure scope or unrestricted file distribution before the parties confirm the applicable terms.
