Use Stage Gates to Control NPI Release Decisions

A gate is a decision record, not a meeting title. Each role contributes its own evidence, and unresolved items need an owner and a disposition before the next stage starts.

G0

NPI Kickoff

Buyer / R&D
Confirm specification revision, quantity stage and target validation milestone.
Engineering
Identify missing inputs, assumptions and the review scope.
Quality / Manufacturing
Flag inspection, process and capacity questions early.

Exit: gate plan, owners and data-gap list.

G1

Prototype Exit

Buyer / R&D
Confirm whether the agreed prototype acceptance targets are met.
Engineering
Compare measured results with targets and record deviations.
Quality / Manufacturing
Review DFM and assembly risks before a first article is planned.

Exit: deviation list and a first-article decision.

G2

FAI / Pilot Exit

Buyer / R&D
Review deviations and any project-specific approval points.
Engineering
Assess RF and mechanical deltas against the baseline.
Quality / Manufacturing
Check inspection, traceability, process and assembly findings.

Exit: open-item disposition and a release recommendation.

G3

Production Release

Buyer / R&D
Confirm the approved revision and change interface for the project.
Engineering
Verify the drawing, BOM, process and test baseline are aligned.
Quality / Manufacturing
Confirm inspection, traceability and records are defined.

Exit: approved production baseline. Residual risk is recorded, not erased.

Open-item rule: Every unresolved finding needs a stated impact, owner, planned action and whether it blocks release. A gate can pass with open items when that disposition is explicitly accepted.

Link First Article Records to the Approved Reference Sample

FAI and a golden sample serve different purposes. One records conformance to requirements; the other is an approved physical reference for comparison. Neither can replace the full inspection and test record.

FAI record fields; characteristics, methods and sample scope come from the controlled drawing and project plan.
FieldRecord content
Characteristic IDUnique reference tied to the drawing or specification requirement.
Requirement and revisionNominal, tolerance and the controlled drawing or BOM revision.
Method and equipmentMeasurement or inspection method, applicable setup and equipment information.
ResultMeasured value or inspection finding, with units where applicable.
DispositionAccept, reject or deviation with the required follow-up.

Golden sample linkage

Record the product identifier, drawing and BOM revision, approval status and any agreed RF or mechanical reference data. Storage, useful life and re-approval are project decisions, not a company-wide policy. A sample without a revision link is not a controlled reference.

Establish Process and Test Baselines during the Pilot Run

The pilot should leave behind a usable operating baseline: how the part is made, how it is assembled, how it is measured and how exceptions are handled. A pilot that only produces acceptable samples has not finished the job.

Baseline 01 · Process

Drawing to repeatable method

Index the drawing and BOM revision, critical machining and assembly steps, applicable fixtures or fastening requirements, surface finish and key inspection points. The detailed scope is defined by the product.

Baseline 02 · Test

Parameter to acceptance record

Link parameters, limits, test conditions, method or setup, applicable coverage and report fields. VNA, PIM, reliability or matrix testing is selected according to the product and specification.

Baseline 03 · Issue log

Finding to closure

Record the symptom, affected lot or sample, temporary containment, cause-analysis status, corrective action and retest result. Do not let a final average hide a single-unit deviation.

At Pilot Exit, compare prototype, first-article and pilot evidence. Decide which settings and records enter the production baseline, and which still require an engineering change or additional verification.

Trace Every Engineering Change to Its Verification and Effectivity

A change is only controlled when the old and new requirements, affected hardware, verification decision and switch point are all visible in one record.

No blanket rule: Some changes can be closed by documented engineering rationale; others need dimensional inspection or full RF re-verification. The decision is recorded per change, not inferred from a generic policy.

Evaluate Prototype and Production Timing from Project Inputs

There is no universal NPI calendar. Timing changes with the design state, the process route, the verification scope and the capacity available when the work is planned.

Timing variables that must be evaluated before a prototype or production date is proposed.
InputEffect on timing
Design freeze stateAn open drawing or unresolved interface drives additional review and revision cycles.
Geometry and materialComplex cavities, thin walls or difficult materials change machining and inspection time.
Finish and assemblyPlating, masking, fastening and tuning add process steps and handling.
Quantity and test scopeSample size, parameter coverage and reporting requirements scale the work.
Deviation cyclesA measured miss may require analysis, revision and another build before the gate can close.
Current capacityAvailable machine and test time must be confirmed when the project is scheduled.

The fastest reference of about two weeks applies only to suitable prototypes. The 50+ high-precision CNC machines and capacity above 500,000 units per year describe manufacturing resources; production lead time is still assessed from drawings, quantity, process and capacity.

RF NPI and Production Readiness FAQs

What prototype approval does not prove, how FAI and a golden sample differ, what a pilot exit review needs and how timing should be assessed.

What still needs verification after a prototype passes?

Prototype approval does not by itself demonstrate production repeatability. The next review should confirm controlled drawings and BOM, first article requirements, manufacturing and assembly risks, test settings, coverage, traceability and open deviations. A pilot run can then evaluate whether the proposed process and evidence baseline support the agreed production-release criteria.

How are engineering changes traced during NPI and production?

An engineering change should reference the affected drawing and BOM revisions, reason, parts and production stage. The review records potential RF, mechanical, process and test impacts, plus any required re-verification. Effectivity, inventory or work-in-progress disposition, and customer notification or approval points should be agreed and linked to the project record.

What is the difference between FAI and a golden sample?

First article inspection documents whether specified characteristics of the first article meet the controlled requirements, including applicable measurements and deviations. A golden sample is an approved physical reference linked to a product and revision. It can support comparison, but it does not replace the inspection records, test data or acceptance decisions required for the project.

What should be reviewed before a pilot run is released to production?

Review the agreed drawing and BOM baseline, first article status, pilot results, critical process and assembly settings, test limits and coverage, traceability, and unresolved deviations. Each open item needs a documented disposition and a decision on whether it blocks release. The exact exit criteria and required customer approvals should be defined in the NPI plan.

How long do RF prototyping and NPI take?

There is no universal NPI schedule. The fastest prototype reference is about two weeks, but it is not a general lead-time promise. Actual timing depends on design freeze, geometry, materials, finishing, quantity, test scope, deviation cycles and capacity. Mass-production delivery must be assessed separately from prototype and stage-gate timing.
