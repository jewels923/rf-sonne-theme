Check Engineering Evidence Before the RFQ

Manufacturing and test capabilities tell you what can be examined. They do not mean every product receives every test by default. Ask for the evidence that matches the characteristic you need to control.

  • CNC machiningShows drawing-defined structures can be reviewed for critical geometry and assembly fit.
  • VNA and PIMSupports measurement of agreed RF characteristics under defined conditions.
  • Reliability testingSupports project-specified environmental or duration checks where applicable.
  • Matrix automationSupports multi-port or batch records; it does not imply 100% coverage of every parameter.

Above 40 GHz, mechanical tolerance can reach ±0.01 mm; above 60 GHz, plating-thickness control can reach 1 μm. Both statements depend on structure and are confirmed during drawing review.

Prepare an Application Review

A useful application review converts a deployment description into a routed product line, a missing-input list and a proposed validation scope. It is an engineering step, not an automatic quotation.

  1. Constraint captureInputApplication, site, band plan, system position, interface and envelope.OutputConstraint sheet with TBD items marked.
  2. System-position checkInputBlock diagram or description of the radio, combining or distribution path.OutputConfirmed functional position and neighbouring interfaces.
  3. Product-line routingInputConstraint sheet plus candidate line from the routing matrix.OutputOne or more candidate lines with open model questions.
  4. Missing-input listInputDraft specification, drawings or link sketch.OutputItems that block feasibility, quotation or validation planning.
  5. Project engineering reviewInputComplete RFQ with agreed revisions.OutputInitial engineering feedback and proposed next step.

Materials worth attaching: band plan, link or coverage sketch, interface drawing, mechanical drawing, target quantity and validation requirements. A complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. That feedback is not a quotation, sample schedule or production delivery date.

Application Selection FAQs

Direct answers on routing, submitting an incomplete application and what happens after engineering review.

How do I know which SONNE product line fits my application?

Start with the system location and constraint, not a part name. Identify the band plan, interface, mechanical envelope, and the issue you need to control, such as isolation, PIM, polarization, or outdoor protection. The application routing table then points to candidate product lines for project-specific engineering review.

Can I submit an application before I have final part numbers?

Yes. An application review can begin with your deployment scenario, frequency range, system interface, estimated quantity, and known constraints. Mark unresolved items as TBD. SONNE can identify missing inputs and candidate product lines, while final specifications and acceptance criteria must be confirmed before quotation or production decisions.

Does SONNE offer one standard solution for every network?

No. Applications may share component families, but frequency plans, interfaces, power, PIM, mechanical limits, and verification requirements differ. SONNE uses standard product capabilities where appropriate and evaluates custom requirements by topology and validation needs. Final suitability is established against the project specification rather than a universal solution claim.

Which application areas are covered on this solutions hub?

The hub covers 5G base stations and RRU, microwave backhaul, satellite earth stations, DAS and in-building coverage, FTTX fiber distribution, and equipment enclosures. Each path starts with deployment constraints and then links to relevant RF, waveguide, combiner, passive, fiber-distribution, or precision-enclosure product lines.

What happens after I describe my application?

The engineering team reviews the application, system position, available specifications, and requested validation. You may receive questions, a candidate product-line route, or a list of missing inputs. A complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback within about 24 hours; this is not a guaranteed quotation, sample lead time, or production delivery date.
