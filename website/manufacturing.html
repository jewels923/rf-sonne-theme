Tolerance, Plating and Surface-Control Evidence

A tolerance statement becomes useful only when the drawing characteristic, applicable structure, measurement method and record are defined together. The figures below are conditional capabilities, not blanket guarantees.

Feature, condition, method and record for high-frequency tolerance and plating review.
Feature and conditionCapability statementMeasurement methodRecord expected

Plating thickness above 60 GHzCan reach 1 μm control for suitable designsAgreed thickness measurement on the specified functional surfaceSurface requirement, result and acceptance status

Reading a tolerance or plating record

  • Which drawing characteristic and revision the value belongs to
  • Which datum scheme and measurement location were used
  • Which method or instrument produced the result
  • Whether the value is a capability statement or an inspection result
  • What deviation, if any, was accepted and by whom

Measurement records are redacted for review; customer drawings and names are not published. Where a result depends on a specific instrument, fixture or sample location, that condition is stated with the value rather than separated from it.

The connection to RF evidence is defined separately: geometry and plating records support the hardware definition, while VNA, PIM or reliability results provide performance evidence for the agreed test plan.

Conditional capability: ±0.01 mm and 1 μm are not universal figures. They apply only to suitable structures, within the stated frequency contexts, and require a drawing requirement plus an agreed verification method.

Assembly, Tuning and RF Verification

Machined parts become an RF assembly through incoming checks, controlled fastening, tuning and test. Each step should leave a record that points back to the build revision and forward to the acceptance decision.

Step 01 · Incoming and assembly

Part condition and build

Check received parts against the revision, then assemble with the specified fasteners, torque or fixture requirements. Record any fit or surface finding before closing the unit.

Step 02 · Fastening and tuning

Repeatable settings

Use the applicable fastening and tuning method, retaining the settings that belong in the process baseline. Automation supports consistency; it does not replace the inspection plan.

Step 03 · RF verification

Measured evidence

VNA, PIM, reliability or matrix testing is selected by product and specification. The result is recorded with conditions and limits; coverage is defined by the project plan.

Applicable testing is not presented as 100% coverage for every product. The released control plan defines which characteristics are measured, at which stage and on which samples. Use the checklist to prepare questions and evidence requests before a visit. Machine count alone does not establish capability; the review should connect equipment, process, inspection and traceability to the product under consideration.

Manufacturing Capability FAQs

Direct answers on tolerance, plating control, the machining-to-test link, capacity and the evidence to request before an audit.

What machining tolerance can SONNE achieve for high-frequency parts?

For suitable structures above 40 GHz, mechanical tolerances can reach ±0.01 mm. This is not a blanket tolerance for every dimension or product. Engineering must review the geometry, material, datum scheme and inspection method, then define measurable drawing requirements and supporting evidence.

How is plating thickness controlled for RF components?

For suitable designs above 60 GHz, plating-thickness control can reach 1 μm, depending on structure and verification. Buyers should specify functional surfaces and acceptance criteria, then agree how thickness and related performance will be recorded. The figure is not a universal guarantee for every coating or component.

How are machining, assembly and RF testing connected?

Machining and surface records should connect to drawing revisions and critical characteristics; assembly and testing should connect to the corresponding build or lot. Applicable VNA, PIM, reliability or matrix-switch tests then provide performance evidence. Exact inspections and coverage are agreed by product and project, not assumed universally.

Does 500,000+ annual capacity guarantee my production lead time?

No. The 500,000+ figure is public annual capacity, not a delivery guarantee for any part. Production lead time must be assessed from drawings, quantity, process route, verification requirements and available capacity. Approximately two weeks is only a reference for applicable prototypes, not a general prototype or production promise.

What evidence should I request before a factory audit?

Request a relevant equipment list, drawing-to-process flow, inspection and calibration records, sample test reports, and lot or serial traceability where applicable. Sensitive customer information should be redacted. Audit scope, evidence access and any on-site agenda should be agreed for the project rather than inferred from machine count alone.

More detail on measurement and reporting is available under testing and quality; production stage records are described under NPI.
