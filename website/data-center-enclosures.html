Match the Enclosure Structure to the Equipment Interface

Different equipment problems call for different mechanical outputs. Use the four categories below to identify the dominant interface, then confirm the actual envelope, mounting and service access from the drawing package.

Start with the installed equipment, not the box. Define the rack or mounting interface, the space reserved for cables and service access, and the direction of airflow. Those three constraints usually decide whether a rack cabinet, a standalone equipment enclosure, an RF housing or a heatsink assembly is the right starting structure.

Industrial installations add vibration, dust, wash-down or outdoor exposure questions. RF modules add connector positions, compartment walls and cover fastening. Mark every unknown as TBD so the review can separate a missing input from a design conflict.

Review Shielding and Thermal Trade-Offs before Design Freeze

Vents, seams, covers and heat paths compete for the same surfaces. Resolve the priority order before the enclosure geometry is released, otherwise a late vent or gasket change can reopen the whole layout.

Input

Operating constraints

Heat load in watts, allowable temperature rise or device limit, airflow direction, forbidden opening zones, shielding objective, service method and target environment.

Trade-off

Three design directions

Solid wall with a conduction path, vented wall with a defined duct, or a local shield compartment. Each changes the thermal path, seam continuity, space and machining.

Output

Decision record

Interface sketch, critical opening and joint list, thermal and shielding risks, plus the items that need measurement or project verification.

Turn Drawings into an Inspection and Assembly Baseline

A quotation needs more than an outside shape. Acceptance depends on datums, critical features, material, finish, fasteners and the assembly relationship between parts. Build those into one controlled drawing set before first article.

Drawing package for a mechanical RFQ

  • 2D PDF with datums, critical dimensions and GD&T
  • 3D model for geometry and interface checking
  • BOM, material and surface-finish specification
  • Fastener schedule and assembly relationship
  • Protection target, cable entry and service access
  • Quantity stage and target validation date

If only a 3D model exists, the review can still start. The output will identify the missing acceptance information before a drawing-based quotation or first-article plan is defined.

Control the Path from Prototype to Production Changes

An approved sample is a design milestone, not a production release. Each stage answers a different question and needs its own record, otherwise a later drawing or process change has no baseline to compare against.

STAGE 01

Prototype

Input: design version and validation target. Review: fit, assembly and agreed RF or thermal behavior. Exit: prototype results and deviations documented.

STAGE 02

First Article

Input: controlled drawing and BOM. Review: critical dimensions, material, finish and assembly. Exit: FAI record and deviation disposition.

STAGE 03

Pilot

Input: first-article disposition and proposed process. Review: repeatability, assembly, test coverage and open issues. Exit: pilot summary and production open items.

STAGE 04

Controlled Production

Input: approved baseline. Control: agreed production, inspection and change rules. Exit: production records retained against the baseline.

Enclosure Engineering FAQs

Short answers to the conflicts that most often appear before a mechanical RFQ: shielding versus cooling, drawing completeness, IP scope, heatsink inputs and change control.

How can EMI shielding and thermal management be balanced in one enclosure?

Start with the heat load, airflow path, allowable openings, shielding objectives and maintenance constraints. SONNE can review conductive paths, joints, covers, vents and compartment layouts as related design choices. The appropriate balance and required verification depend on the equipment specification; neither EMI nor thermal performance should be assumed from enclosure material alone.

Is a 3D model enough for an enclosure quotation or design review?

A 3D model is useful for evaluating geometry, space and interfaces, but it usually does not define all acceptance requirements. Also provide available 2D drawings, materials, finishes, critical dimensions, GD&T, fasteners, quantities and application conditions. If information is missing, SONNE can identify open items before defining the next review or quotation step.

Can SONNE provide IP55 or IP65 telecom cabinets?

IP55 and IP65 are within the enclosure capability information provided for project evaluation. The achievable rating depends on the complete cabinet design, seals, joints, cable entries and agreed verification method. Share the installation environment and target rating so the requirements can be reviewed; do not treat a capability statement as certification for every custom enclosure.

What information is needed for a custom CNC heatsink review?

Provide the heat source and dissipation target, available envelope, mounting interface, airflow conditions, material preference, surface requirements, critical tolerances and production stage. Include 2D and 3D files when available. These inputs allow the machining and thermal interfaces to be reviewed together; final geometry and verification requirements remain project-specific.

How are enclosure design changes controlled after sample approval?

Changes should be linked to the controlled drawing and BOM revision, with the reason, affected parts and review result recorded. Depending on the change, the review may cover fit, machining, finish, shielding, thermal interfaces and renewed verification. Customer notification or approval points, inventory disposition and the effective production stage should be agreed for the specific project.
