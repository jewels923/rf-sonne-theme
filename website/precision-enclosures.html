A 3D model can start a preliminary review, but geometry alone does not define tolerances, material, appearance, inspection or assembly acceptance. Those items must be completed for a reliable quotation.

Upload and requirement checklist

  • Critical dimensions, tolerances and GD&T

Thermal inputs

Heat dissipation, allowable temperature rise, ambient temperature, airflow and mounting orientation.

Environment inputs

Indoor or outdoor installation, cable entries, maintenance conditions and target IP capability direction.

EMI inputs

Shielding constraints, seams, apertures, connector openings, conductive interfaces and service access.

IP55, IP65 and IP68 are capability directions only. Any applicable rating must be tied to the exact structure, intended use conditions and available test evidence; none is a blanket certification for every cabinet or housing. RoHS, REACH, salt-spray and EMC compliance are not claimed here and require separate project-level confirmation and evidence. Discuss EMI / Thermal Requirements.

Control Prototype, Pilot Production and Drawing Changes

Prototype approval is not the end of production definition. Carry drawing revisions, first-article and assembly findings, pilot baselines and ECN decisions into controlled production.

  1. Drawing review

    Input: 2D/3D package and open requirements.
    Output: Review comments and controlled open-item list.

  2. Prototype

    Input: Prototype drawing and acceptance needs.
    Output: Prototype and project-agreed findings.

  3. First article / assembly

    Input: Critical features and mating definition.
    Output: Agreed FAI and assembly confirmation records.

  4. Pilot production

    Input: Approved baseline and pilot requirements.
    Output: Process, inspection and Golden Sample baseline as agreed.

  5. Controlled production

    Input: Released revision and change status.
    Output: Production and inspection records under ECN control.

06 / Drawing notes3 topics · 5 questions

Precision Enclosure Questions before You Send Drawings

Direct answers on preliminary review, shielding and thermal trade-offs, finishes and protection, OEM customization and project timing.

Drawings

Can I request an enclosure quote with only a 3D model?

A 3D model is enough to begin an engineering review, but a reliable quotation normally also needs material, quantity, critical dimensions and tolerances, surface finish, assembly requirements and the drawing revision. Send STEP plus a 2D PDF when available. Missing items can be listed as open assumptions instead of being silently guessed.

Performance

How can EMI shielding and thermal management be balanced?

Review seams, apertures, connector openings, conductive interfaces, heat sources, airflow and service access together. The right balance depends on shielding targets, heat dissipation, allowable temperature rise and the installation environment. SONNE can assess these inputs with the mechanical design; any required EMC or thermal compliance must be confirmed and evidenced for the specific project.

Which enclosure finishes and IP ratings are available?

SONNE's published capabilities include powder coating and galvanizing, with IP55, IP65 and IP68 capability referenced for relevant enclosure projects. The available finish and achievable rating depend on the design, material, sealing method and use conditions. Request the applicable test evidence rather than assuming every cabinet or housing carries every rating.

Production

Can SONNE support custom OEM enclosure dimensions and assembly requirements?

Yes, the enclosure line is positioned for custom OEM/ODM projects. Submit overall dimensions, equipment layout, mounting interfaces, cable entries, access requirements, finish, quantity and applicable environmental targets. Feasibility, branding details, packaging and production terms are reviewed per project; no cross-line commercial policy should be assumed before confirmation.

How long do enclosure prototypes and production take?

A prototype in as fast as about two weeks is only a reference for suitable projects, not a general promise. Actual sample and volume schedules depend on drawing maturity, materials, quantity, processes, surface treatment and current capacity. A complete RFQ normally receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours, followed by a project-specific schedule assessment.
