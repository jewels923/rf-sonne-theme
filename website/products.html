07 / Selection notes5 questions

Product Line Selection FAQs

Navigation-level answers for supply scope, customization and RFQ routing. Detailed electrical requirements belong in the relevant product and engineering review.

Which SONNE product lines are core product lines?

The portfolio is organised in two groups. Five passive component lines cover RF and microwave filters, waveguide and microwave components, combiners and duplexers, base-station RF, and precision enclosures. Five system and active RF lines cover antenna test systems, switch matrices, T/R modules, high-power passive microwave components, and RF front-ends. IBS/DAS and FTTX are supporting product lines, while RF accessories provide additional project-completion items.

Can SONNE manufacture non-standard RF components?

SONNE supports custom evaluation across its listed RF, microwave, waveguide, combiner and enclosure product lines. Feasibility depends on the requested frequency, topology, performance, mechanical interface and validation requirements. Submit a specification, band plan or drawing for engineering review; a non-standard request is not automatically treated as a confirmed manufacturable design.

How do I choose the right product line?

Start with the function you need: filtering, waveguide transmission or polarization, band combining or separation, base-station RF integration, or equipment enclosure. If you know only the application, use the application navigator. For a project spanning several lines, select all relevant categories in the RFQ and upload one BOM or system requirement.

What frequency range does the product portfolio cover?

The core production portfolio covers DC to 40 GHz. Selected custom microwave and waveguide designs can reach up to 110 GHz, subject to topology and validation. This does not mean every product line or model covers the full range. Check the relevant category and submit your exact frequency band for confirmation.

Can I request a quote before selecting a specific model?

Yes. You can submit an application, target specification, band plan, drawing or BOM without choosing a model. Identify the product line if known and include expected quantities and key constraints. Engineering can then determine the appropriate review path. A complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours, not a guaranteed quotation or delivery date.
