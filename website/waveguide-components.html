06 / Project gatesBand plan to production

From Band Plan and Interfaces to a Validated Waveguide Design

RF and mechanical reviews run together from the first gate, reducing the chance that an electrically discussed concept later fails the flange, polarization or envelope requirement.

  1. Buyer inputs

    Check band plan, flange, polarization, envelope and application.
    Output: Known boundaries and open items.

  2. RF / mechanical review

    Evaluate function, topology, interfaces and structure together.
    Output: Feasibility questions and review path.

  3. Prototype & VNA validation

    Define ports, calibration/reference plane and acceptance targets.
    Output: Prototype and agreed RF evidence.

  4. Pilot confirmation

    Confirm drawing, process and test baseline.
    Output: Project-specific production baseline.

  5. Production

    Execute controlled requirements and project-agreed verification.
    Output: Applicable production records.

A complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. Design conclusions, quotations and delivery timing require project review. The fastest prototype reference is about two weeks only as a reference, not a uniform commitment for E-band, OMT or OCU projects.

07 / Component notes5 questions

Waveguide Component and Customization FAQs

Clarify OMT and OCU functions, E-band inputs, flange and polarization definitions, frequency boundaries and drawing-based evaluation.

What is the difference between an OMT and an OCU?

An OMT, or ortho-mode transducer, separates or combines orthogonal polarizations in a waveguide interface. An OCU is an outdoor combining unit used to combine signal paths in an outdoor link assembly. They solve different system functions, so their RF inputs, interfaces and validation requirements should be specified separately.

What parameters are required for an E-band waveguide evaluation?

Provide the device function, exact transmit and receive bands, bandwidth, insertion loss, isolation or rejection targets, power, waveguide and flange interfaces, mechanical envelope and outdoor requirements. Include polarization details where relevant, plus expected quantity and test requirements. A band plan and interface drawing help engineering assess feasibility without assuming missing values.

How should flange and polarization requirements be defined?

Identify the waveguide size, flange type or interface drawing, port orientation, sealing or mating constraints and mechanical envelope. For polarization-sensitive hardware, define each port, polarization direction and the performance to be verified. If any item is unknown, mark it for review rather than allowing the supplier to assume an interface.

What frequency range can custom waveguide components reach?

SONNE lists OCU customization from 6 to 80 GHz and selected custom designs up to 110 GHz, depending on topology and validation. These figures do not establish one universal range for every waveguide component. Submit the exact device function and band plan so engineering can confirm the applicable design and test boundary.

Can SONNE evaluate waveguide assemblies from drawings?

Yes, custom waveguide assemblies can be evaluated from 2D or 3D drawings together with the operating frequency, waveguide and flange interfaces, material or plating requirements, quantity and validation needs. RF performance cannot be inferred from geometry alone, so include the required electrical targets and test conditions whenever they apply.
