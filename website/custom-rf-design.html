Define the RF Evaluation Inputs and Outputs

An evaluation is only as useful as its input table. Record electrical targets, mechanical limits and project context with units and test conditions, then agree what the review will return.

RF inputs
  • Center frequency, start and stop frequencies
  • Bandwidth and channel plan
  • Ports, impedance and connector or flange type
  • Insertion loss, return loss, rejection and isolation targets
  • Power handling and PIM where applicable
  • Test conditions and calibration assumptions
Mechanical and project inputs
  • Envelope, mounting orientation and available volume
  • Material, surface finish and plating requirements
  • Operating environment and interface constraints
  • Available 2D/3D data and reference drawings
  • Quantity stage and target validation milestone
  • Known deviations or issues in existing hardware
Review outputs
  • Confirmed requirements and TBD items
  • Documented assumptions and priority order
  • Topology or structure comments
  • Manufacturing and DFM risks
  • Proposed prototype scope
  • Verification matrix and next gate

The output is a review record, not a promise that every open item closes in one pass. Unknown fields stay visible so the buyer can decide whether to supply data, accept an assumption or change the target. A standard input template is available through the RFQ guide.

Move from Design Analysis to Hardware through Review Gates

Each gate has a defined question and a record. Analysis can guide the next build, but it is never presented as measured hardware performance.

  1. G1 · Design Review

    Check the requirement baseline, model boundaries, critical structural assumptions and target trade-offs. Output the assumptions that still need validation and the design review record.

  2. G1b · DFM Review

    Review critical dimensions, machining reach, mating faces, connectors or flanges, tolerances and finish against one revision. Output manufacturability and risk comments.

  3. G2 · Prototype Build

    Freeze the drawing, BOM and test requirements used for the sample, then record actual deviations. The fastest reference of about two weeks applies only to suitable prototypes; timing follows structure, process and verification.

  4. G2 · Validation Review

    Compare targets with applicable VNA or PIM measurements under agreed conditions. Unmet items enter root-cause discussion and the next review instead of being declared passed.

Gate discipline: A design comment, a simulated curve and a measured sample are three different evidence types. Each record identifies which one it contains so the buyer's team can see what remains open.

Close the Loop between RF Results and Physical Design

When measured results miss a target, the useful next step is a structured comparison, not another blind build. Connect the delta to a physical hypothesis, revise one controlled variable and retest on a comparable setup.

  1. Record the resultParameter, target, test conditions, sample result and the difference.
  2. Link a hypothesisCavity dimension, joint, connector, flange, tolerance or plating as a possible contributor.
  3. Revise one versionChange the drawing or model with a reason and a new revision identifier.
  4. Retest and compareRepeat on a comparable setup and retain before/after data for review.

Causality boundary: A correlation between a measured shift and a physical feature is a review hypothesis until the data supports it. SONNE does not state a root cause without measurement evidence.

Custom RF Design Evaluation FAQs

What to send, how far the frequency range extends, what a review returns, and how initial feedback differs from a formal quotation or delivery commitment.

What parameters are needed for a custom RF design evaluation?

Provide the frequency range or band plan, bandwidth, ports and impedance, insertion loss, return loss, rejection or isolation targets, power and PIM where applicable. Add connector or flange details, mechanical envelope, environment, drawings and project stage. Include test conditions with critical targets; unknown items can be listed as open for review.

Can SONNE participate in design evaluation instead of only machining to drawing?

Yes, engineering evaluation is part of the stated custom RF design capability for filter, waveguide and combiner projects. The review can connect RF requirements with mechanical and manufacturing constraints, identify open questions and define prototype verification. The exact scope depends on the supplied information and agreed project objectives, and remains subject to hardware validation.

What frequency range can a custom RF project cover?

SONNE's standard product portfolio is centered on production from DC to 40 GHz. Selected custom microwave and waveguide projects can reach 110 GHz, depending on topology and verification. Frequency alone is not enough to confirm feasibility; the design review must also consider interfaces, performance targets, mechanical structure, manufacturing constraints and the applicable test method.

What outputs should I expect from an RF engineering review?

Expected review outputs can include a requirement and open-item list, documented assumptions, RF and mechanical risks, design or DFM comments, and a proposed prototype verification matrix. The agreed output set should be confirmed for the project. Simulation or design analysis should be identified separately from measured hardware evidence, so your team can see what remains to be validated.

How quickly can SONNE provide initial engineering feedback?

A complete RFQ can typically receive initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. This refers to an initial response, such as identifying missing inputs or suggesting the next review step; it is not a guaranteed quotation, completed design or delivery commitment. Prototype and production timing must be assessed from the design, process, quantity and verification scope.
