What Happens Next

Your request moves through a short review sequence. Each step is about understanding the requirement before any technical or commercial answer is given.

  1. STEP 01

    The team reviews your information

    The application, product line, frequency or mechanical requirements and project stage are checked against the relevant engineering workstream.

  2. STEP 02

    You may receive clarifying questions

    If a key input is missing or ambiguous, the team may ask a short follow-up. Values you do not know can stay open and be marked as unresolved.

  3. STEP 03

    Initial engineering feedback follows

    A complete RFQ can typically receive initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. It identifies feasibility questions and next steps, not a finished quotation or delivery commitment.

If your request is incomplete, the first reply will identify what is missing and how to close the gap rather than estimate a price from partial information.

Prepare These Six Items for the Review

You do not need a perfect package to continue the conversation. These six items let the team review the requirement once instead of asking for the same details in several rounds.

Send what you already have and mark the rest as open. The RFQ guide explains each input, and the engineering pages show how design, testing and manufacturing are reviewed.

Open the RFQ checklist  →
  • 1. ApplicationWhere the part will be used, such as a 5G RRU, microwave backhaul link, earth station, DAS project or equipment enclosure.
  • 2. Product lineFilters, waveguide components, combiners or duplexers, base-station RF, precision enclosures or accessories.
  • 3. Key frequency or mechanical requirementsBands and bandwidth, insertion loss, isolation, power, PIM, envelope, connectors or critical dimensions.
  • 4. Quantity or stageSample, prototype, pilot or production volume, plus the timing you are planning around.
  • 5. AttachmentsBand plan, specification, 2D or 3D drawings, existing test data and the revision you are working from.
  • 6. Question to resolveThe decision or blocker you want the review to answer, such as topology fit, tolerance feasibility or test coverage.
