A Practical Path from RFQ to Repeatable Production

The working model is deliberately narrow: agree the requirement, verify a sample, then confirm what production will repeat. Commercial terms and production timing are settled per project, not assumed from a company page.

  1. Requirement intake

    Your input
    Application, band plan, 2D/3D drawings, quantity range and open questions.
    SONNE activity
    Completeness check and routing to the responsible engineer.
    Review output
    List of missing inputs and the questions that block evaluation.

  2. Engineering evaluation

    Your input
    Priority of RF targets and non-negotiable mechanical constraints.
    SONNE activity
    Feasibility, topology, tolerance and test-scope review.
    Review output
    Initial engineering feedback; a complete RFQ typically receives this in about 24 hours.

  3. Sample verification

    Your input
    Acceptance targets, conditions and the measurements you need reported.
    SONNE activity
    Prototype build, tuning and measurement against the agreed limits.
    Review output
    Sample data with deviations and their disposition. The fastest prototype reference is about two weeks, only in suitable cases.

  4. Pilot and volume

    Your input
    Target volume, acceptance criteria and any change constraints.
    SONNE activity
    Process route, capacity and inspection planning.
    Review output
    Production baseline, open deviations and the change interface.

  5. Agreed deliverables

    Your input
    Required report fields, traceability depth and delivery documents.
    SONNE activity
    Project test plan and record keeping.
    Review output
    Records limited to the agreed product, lot and test scope.

24-hour guideline: a complete RFQ typically receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours. This is not a quotation, a delivery date or a technical proposal guarantee.

Timing: the approximately two-week reference applies to prototyping in suitable cases only. Production timing is assessed from drawings, quantity, process and current capacity.

Questions Buyers Ask About SONNE

Five questions that appear in most supplier shortlists, answered with the same limits used elsewhere on this site. Each answer points to the page where the claim can be checked.

Is SONNE a factory or a trading company?

SONNE is an engineering and manufacturing company founded in 2015, with a manufacturing base in Cixi, Ningbo, China. Its production capabilities include more than 50 high-precision CNC machines and RF testing resources. You can review factory photographs, machining, assembly and testing areas on the Factory page before arranging a project-specific audit or visit.

Where is SONNE based?

SONNE's manufacturing base is at No. 87, 89, 91, Sanbei Middle Road, Xinze Village, Guanhaiwei Town, Cixi City, Ningbo City, Zhejiang Province, China. Visitors should arrange a factory visit in advance through the Contact page so the team can confirm the schedule, site access and the people you need to meet.

Does SONNE support custom RF and enclosure projects?

Yes. SONNE supports custom RF and microwave filters, waveguide components, combiners, base-station RF hardware and precision enclosure projects. Core production covers DC to 40 GHz, while selected custom microwave and waveguide designs may reach 110 GHz depending on topology and verification. Feasibility, documentation and test scope are evaluated against each project's requirements.

What do SONNE's capacity figures mean for a buyer?

More than 50 high-precision CNC machines and annual capacity above 500,000 units indicate manufacturing resources for prototype-to-volume evaluation. They do not create a universal lead-time promise for every design. Your schedule still depends on drawings, quantities, processes and current capacity, so the project team should confirm timing after reviewing the actual RFQ.

How does SONNE support quality verification?

SONNE has an ISO 9001 quality system and VNA, PIM, reliability and multi-port matrix automated testing capabilities. These resources support verification according to the product and agreed test scope. Buyers should review the Testing and Quality page and request project-relevant evidence rather than treating the company-level capability list as a blanket test claim for every component.
