Control Machining, Plating and Waveguide Interfaces

At E-band, small geometry and surface changes matter. The review therefore connects drawing characteristics, machining process, plating control and VNA evidence instead of treating them as separate promises.

  • Cavity geometryCritical dimensions, datums and internal surfaces reviewed against the drawing.
  • Flange interfaceHole pattern, sealing face and mating orientation checked before machining.
  • Plating controlSurface specification and thickness control discussed for the actual structure.
  • AssemblyFastening, alignment and interface torque considered in the process review.

Above 40 GHz, mechanical tolerance can reach ±0.01 mm; above 60 GHz, plating-thickness control can reach 1 μm. Both are conditional capabilities that depend on structure and drawing review, not universal guarantees.

Specify Outdoor Integration and Validation Separately

Weather protection, tuning-free design and project scheduling are three different questions. Keeping them separate prevents one from being read as a promise for the others.

  1. Structure reviewInput: mating surfaces, sealing method, materials, fasteners and drainage path.Output: a list of design items to confirm and any required environmental test conditions.
  2. Prototype and fitInput: machined parts, assembly sequence, interface drawings and sample targets.Output: fit findings, assembly notes and open deviations before electrical validation.
  3. VNA and agreed environment testsInput: port list, frequency ranges, limits and any environmental test requirement.Output: measurements and condition records tied to the sample revision.
  4. Acceptance baselineInput: agreed limits, coverage, report format and traceability fields.Output: the baseline used for production release, not a blanket approval for every variant.

Tuning-free, defined

SONNE uses tuning-free to describe a design and manufacturing path that reduces manual adjustment through controlled geometry and processes. It does not mean that every structure needs no tuning, and it does not remove VNA verification.

Tuning and delivery timing are evaluated from design maturity, structure, quantity and validation scope. The fastest approximately two-week reference applies only to prototyping in suitable cases.

Environmental claims: IP, salt-spray, EMC or other compliance statements are confirmed for the specific design and test condition. They are not assumed from a product photograph or a general outdoor description.

Microwave Backhaul FAQs

Tuning time, outdoor protection, 71–86 GHz diplexers, required inputs and what tuning-free does and does not mean.

How long does E-band filter or diplexer tuning take?

There is no responsible universal tuning-time figure. Timing depends on design maturity, topology, machining results, frequency range, and validation scope. A tuning-free design path may reduce manual adjustment, but it does not remove electrical verification. SONNE should review the drawings and acceptance requirements before confirming a project schedule.

How is outdoor protection handled for a microwave OCU?

Outdoor protection is defined around the actual assembly: mating interfaces, sealing method, materials or plating, fasteners, drainage where relevant, and the required environmental test conditions. Provide the installation and specification requirements for review. IP, salt-spray, EMC, or other compliance claims must be confirmed for the specific design rather than assumed.

Can SONNE evaluate a 71–86 GHz waveguide diplexer?

Yes, E-band 71–86 GHz is part of the stated backhaul application scope. Feasibility still depends on the exact transmit and receive sub-bands, guard band, insertion loss, isolation, return loss, power, flange, and mechanical envelope. Selected custom microwave and waveguide projects can reach 110 GHz, subject to topology and validation.

What inputs are needed for a backhaul diplexer evaluation?

Provide transmit and receive frequency ranges, bandwidth, guard band, rejection and isolation targets, insertion loss, return loss, power, waveguide size, flange, port orientation, mechanical limits, and environmental requirements. Include system and interface drawings where available. Unknown items can be marked TBD for engineering clarification before final feasibility review.

Does tuning-free mean that no RF testing is required?

No. Tuning-free refers to a design and manufacturing approach intended to reduce manual adjustment through controlled geometry and processes. It does not eliminate the need to verify the agreed electrical characteristics or production consistency. The applicable VNA measurements, acceptance limits, and test coverage should be defined for each design and order.
