Prepare Your Interface Specification

Map the Feed, OMT and Waveguide Chain

The OMT separates polarizations; a diplexer separates transmit and receive frequencies; waveguide sections carry and turn the signal. They are different functions and should not be described with one generic isolation figure.

  1. Antenna feedReceives both polarizations and presents them to the OMT. Feed geometry is defined by the antenna design.
  2. OMTSeparates or combines orthogonal polarizations into defined ports. Requires a port and polarization map.Waveguide components →
  3. Diplexer or filterSeparates transmit and receive bands after polarization handling. Requires Tx/Rx edges and guard band.RF filters →
  4. Waveguide runStraight, curved, twisted or transition sections connect the feed assembly to the equipment.Waveguide assemblies →

OMT and OCU are not interchangeable. An OMT handles polarization; an outdoor combining unit in a backhaul link handles transmit and receive combining. Each has its own port definition and test conditions.

Define Your Acceptance Priorities

Control Flanges, Datums and High-Frequency Geometry

Custom waveguide work starts with a drawing review. Flange details, datum choices and internal geometry decide both fit and electrical behaviour, so they are checked before machining is planned.

  • Flange specificationStandard or drawing, hole pattern and bolt access.
  • Sealing faceSurface finish and groove details where a seal is required.
  • Waveguide boreInternal dimensions, transitions and surface requirement.
  • Polarization datumReference feature and orientation relative to ports.
  • GD&TCritical dimensions, datums and inspection method.
  • Assembly directionMating order and access for fastening.

Above 40 GHz, mechanical tolerance can reach ±0.01 mm; above 60 GHz, plating-thickness control can reach 1 μm. Both capabilities depend on the structure and are confirmed in drawing review rather than applied uniformly to every part.

Agree on VNA Evidence and Acceptance Criteria

Define what the report must prove before samples are measured. The report format, port list, frequency ranges and limits should be agreed with the same revision that will be manufactured.

  • Product identityPart or assembly identifier, drawing revision and serial or lot linkage.
  • Port mapWhich ports were measured and how they correspond to the polarization scheme.
  • Frequency rangeStart, stop and any sub-band limits used for acceptance.
  • Test configurationCalibration reference, fixtures, terminations and environmental condition where relevant.
  • S-parameter setReturn loss, insertion loss and isolation as defined for this assembly.
  • Limits and resultsAcceptance values, measured data and pass or deviation disposition.
  • CoverageSample, lot or serial basis for the report; not every test applies to every unit.

Ask before assuming. Polarization-related system measurements, environmental tests and complete electrical coverage are not automatic. Confirm the test method, limits and any system-level measurement for the specific OMT or assembly.

Satellite Earth Station RF FAQs

OMT polarization verification, drawing-based manufacture, Ku/Ka scope, flange inputs and the evidence that can accompany a component.

How is OMT polarization performance verified?

First define the frequency range, port configuration, polarization scheme, and the exact acceptance parameters. Polarization-related performance, port isolation, return loss, and insertion loss are not interchangeable. SONNE can review the geometry and apply VNA verification where applicable; the final test method, limits, and any system-level measurements must be agreed for the specific OMT.

Can SONNE manufacture OMT and waveguide parts to my drawings?

Yes, custom waveguide assemblies and OMT projects can be evaluated from customer drawings. Provide 2D and 3D files where available, including frequency, ports, polarization, waveguide size, flange, critical dimensions, materials or plating, quantity, and acceptance requirements. Feasibility remains subject to engineering review of the topology, tolerances, and validation scope.

Are Ku-band and Ka-band OMT projects supported?

Ku/Ka band OMT is part of the stated satellite earth-station component scope. Each design still requires confirmation of exact sub-bands, polarization, isolation, return loss, insertion loss, power, waveguide interfaces, and mechanical envelope. Selected custom microwave and waveguide work can extend to 110 GHz, depending on topology and validation rather than frequency alone.

What flange and interface details should I provide?

Provide the waveguide size, flange type or drawing, hole pattern, sealing features, mating interface, port orientation, polarization datum, mechanical envelope, and critical tolerances. Identify any controlled plating or surface requirements and the assembly context. Clear interface data helps engineering review both fit and high-frequency geometry before manufacturing decisions.

What RF test evidence can accompany an earth-station component?

The evidence package is defined per project. It may include agreed VNA measurements, test configuration details, frequency range, acceptance limits, and report records for the specified samples or production coverage. Request the required format during engineering review. Do not assume that every electrical, polarization, environmental, or system-level test is included by default.
