Map the POI to the Indoor Coverage Branches

The POI is the boundary between operator equipment and the passive distribution. From there, splitters, couplers and antennas form branches whose function and connector series must stay consistent.

  1. NODE 01Operator or base-station feedsUplink and downlink paths from each operator enter the system.
  2. NODE 02POI combiningCombines and distributes operator bands onto the shared indoor infrastructure.RF combiners →
  3. NODE 03Floor splitterDivides power to zones. Split ratio and power rating are model inputs.IBS/DAS components →
  4. NODE 04Coupler or hybridTaps a defined portion of the signal or combines two branches with controlled isolation.Couplers →
  5. NODE 05Indoor antennaRadiates into the coverage zone. Mounting and connector complete the branch.RF accessories →

Do not substitute by port count. A hybrid coupler and a directional coupler serve different functions even when both have multiple ports. The branch function decides which one belongs in the BOM.

Loss budget checks

  • Splitter theoretical split plus excess loss
  • Coupler through-loss and tap value
  • Cable and connector loss per run
  • Antenna input power at the last branch

PIM conditions to record

  • Test tones and power per tone
  • Termination and connector type
  • Model and frequency band
  • Acceptance limit and sample basis

DAS and IBS Project FAQs

POI operator count, complete-BOM review, low-PIM scope, 698–3800 MHz coverage and commercial conditions for a mixed BOM.

How many operators can a DAS POI support?

There is no universal operator count for every POI. The feasible configuration depends on the operator band plan, uplink and downlink paths, port architecture, isolation, insertion loss, power, PIM, and mechanical requirements. Submit the bands and interface diagram so the POI or combining approach can be evaluated for the actual multi-operator system.

Can SONNE review and quote a complete DAS passive BOM?

Yes, a project BOM can be reviewed across POI or combining items, low PIM splitters and couplers, hybrid couplers, indoor antennas, and related accessories. Include frequencies, quantities, connectors, power and PIM requirements for each line. Missing or conflicting entries may require clarification before model suitability, pricing, MOQ, and delivery are confirmed.

Does one low PIM rating apply to the entire DAS BOM?

No. PIM performance is model- and test-condition-dependent. A published capability of up to ≤-160 dBc applies only where stated for a specific model, not automatically to every component or the complete BOM. Confirm the rating, power, connectors, acceptance limit, and required verification for each relevant BOM line.

Does the DAS component range cover 698–3800 MHz?

The stated DAS/IBS passive component range includes 698–3800 MHz broadband products. Coverage must still be checked by model and function, including the required sub-bands, port configuration, coupling or split ratio, power, PIM, and connectors. Do not assume that every POI, splitter, coupler, antenna, or accessory spans the entire range.

Is there one MOQ for a complete DAS project?

No universal project MOQ should be assumed across a mixed BOM. Requirements can vary by component, model, customization, and order structure. Send the complete list with quantities so SONNE can confirm the applicable conditions line by line. Any MOQ, quotation, or delivery schedule must be agreed for the specific project rather than inferred from another product line.
