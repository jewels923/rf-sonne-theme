Verify PIM, Isolation and Repeatability beyond One Sample

A single prototype curve cannot establish production repeatability. Define the approved baseline, controlled build conditions and batch evidence together. SONNE's VNA, PIM, reliability and automated multi-port matrix capabilities are applied according to the product and agreed verification plan.

  1. Prototype

    Review: Sample curves, port configuration, test conditions and agreed limits.
    Buyer confirmation: Prototype performance and open design items.

  2. Pilot production

    Review: Approved-sample baseline, materials/assembly controls and initial batch records.
    Buyer confirmation: Process and test baseline.

  3. Controlled production

    Review: Agreed batch tests, traceability and deviation handling.
    Buyer confirmation: Report scope and acceptance records.

06 / RFQ notes5 questions

Base-Station RF Questions before You Submit an RFQ

Review the engineering principles and project-level boundaries before sending your band plan. The answers do not extend published examples into range-wide product commitments.

How do I choose between 4T4R and 8TR base-station RF components?

Start with the radio architecture, number of transmit and receive paths, band plan, port mapping and mechanical envelope rather than choosing by channel count alone. Share these inputs with insertion loss, isolation, power and PIM targets. SONNE can then evaluate a suitable 4T4R or 8TR configuration against the project requirements.

How can low PIM be maintained across production batches?

Batch consistency requires an approved sample baseline, controlled materials and assembly, defined test conditions, and traceable production records. SONNE has PIM, VNA, reliability and automated multi-port testing capabilities. The exact PIM limit, sampling or verification coverage, and report format should be agreed for each base-station product rather than assumed across the range.

What information is needed to address co-siting interference?

Provide the interfering and victim bands, transmit and receive frequencies, power levels, antenna or feeder arrangement, isolation targets and known site constraints. SONNE can review whether a base-station filter, duplexer or co-siting RF configuration is appropriate. Feeder-sharing and generic multi-band combining requirements are routed to the dedicated combiners line for evaluation.

Can SONNE evaluate N28 and N78 base-station projects?

Yes, N28 and N78 project requirements can be submitted for engineering evaluation, with published evidence covering lower-band designs and a 3400–3600 MHz 8TR example. Final feasibility depends on the complete Tx/Rx band plan, performance targets, power, PIM, interfaces and mechanical limits, so no single catalog specification should be assumed for every design.

Can I request a review before my base-station specification is complete?

Yes. Submit the known band plan, architecture, target quantities and open questions, and clearly mark missing values. A complete RFQ normally receives initial engineering feedback in about 24 hours; this is not a quotation or lead-time guarantee. Prototype and production schedules are assessed separately from design complexity, quantity, process and capacity.
