IP68 stops at the closure line. It is a published capability for splice-closure products. It is not a rating for every distribution box, optical cabinet or enclosure, and it does not transfer to another model or entry configuration.

Salt-spray, RoHS, REACH and EMC are not asserted here. Where a project requires them, the applicable standard, test evidence and acceptance criteria are confirmed for the proposed design rather than assumed from the product family.

Move from Custom Drawings to an Agreed Project Baseline

A custom FTTX enclosure is a sequence of agreements, not a single request. Each step closes one source of variation before the next one opens.

  1. Deployment checklist

    Site, function, capacity, cable entries, mounting and envelope are recorded. Unknown items stay marked TBD.

  2. 2D / 3D drawing review

    Interfaces, door or cover geometry, internal hardware and installation clearance are checked against the drawing set.

  3. Material, coating and sealing confirmation

    The proposed material, surface treatment and sealing method are matched to the project specification.

  4. Sample and acceptance baseline

    Sample scope and acceptance items are agreed and written down. A prototype reference of about two weeks applies only where the case suits it.

  5. Quantity and delivery assessment

    Volume, tooling, process and capacity are reviewed before a project schedule is proposed.

OEM inputs to submit

  • Logo or marking files in the required format
  • Placement position and size on the enclosure
  • Colour and surface requirements
  • Packaging and labelling needs
  • Version number and approval reference

Change control

  • Any change to size, entries or hardware returns to the drawing set
  • Acceptance items are re-checked against the new baseline
  • OEM files are versioned before production release
  • Quantity and schedule are re-assessed after the change

Two weeks is a prototype reference, not an FTTX lead time. It applies only to suitable prototype cases. Production and project delivery are assessed from drawings, quantity, process and capacity.

FTTX Distribution and Closure FAQs

IP selection, custom dimensions, the real scope of IP68, OEM branding and outdoor material review.

How should I choose an IP rating for an FTTX enclosure?

Start with the actual installation exposure, sealing interfaces, cable entries, maintenance method, and the operator or project specification. Select the enclosure and closure by function, then request model-specific evidence for the required rating. Do not choose by enclosure appearance alone or assume that one published IP rating covers every FTTX product.

Can FTTX distribution boxes be made to custom dimensions?

Custom enclosure requirements can be evaluated from the deployment specification and drawings. Provide the required capacity, cable entries, mounting method, maintenance access, mechanical envelope, IP target, materials or coating, quantity, and OEM requirements. Final dimensions and feasibility must be agreed through engineering review rather than inferred from a standard product photograph.

Are all SONNE FTTX boxes and closures IP68?

No. IP68 is a published capability for splice-closure products, not a blanket rating for every FTTX distribution box, optical cabinet, or enclosure. Check the exact model, structure, cable-entry configuration, and required test evidence for your project. Other enclosure ratings and compliance requirements must be confirmed individually.

Are custom dimensions and OEM branding available for FTTX projects?

Custom enclosure and OEM branding options are part of the stated FTTX capability. Submit dimension drawings, branding files, placement, color or surface requirements, packaging needs, and quantities for review. The available customization, acceptance baseline, MOQ, pricing, and delivery schedule must be confirmed for the specific project before release.

How are outdoor corrosion and material requirements evaluated?

Provide the installation environment, material or coating specification, expected exposure, and any required test standard. SONNE can review outdoor materials, spraying, enclosure geometry, and sealing requirements for the proposed design. Salt-spray performance or other environmental compliance must not be assumed; applicable evidence and acceptance criteria need project-specific confirmation.
